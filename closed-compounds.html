A proactive bubble around each person, tagged asset and danger area on site.

On an airport apron or any closed site, every person on foot, every vehicle, every tagged asset and every danger area has a proactive bubble. When two bubbles touch, the driver is warned, even out of sight. A parked aircraft, a vehicle or an open pit is marked with small tags and gets its own bubble.

The situation

A tug reverses behind a parked aircraft. A worker is walking behind it.

Neither sees the other. Across the apron, a vehicle heads for the runway hold line. The oldest risk on any site is a driver who cannot see the person on foot, or the line the vehicle must not cross.

12Awareness and understanding

Every phone on site knows who and what is near it.

The worker and the tug are aware of each other, even with the aircraft between them. Each has its own proactive bubble, on a phone people already carry: the worker’s, and the driver’s in the cab.

Today: a parked aircraft, a vehicle or an open pit is marked with small tags and gets its own bubble.

3Prediction

It will see the tug and the worker about to meet, before the worker steps into its path.

Today: the driver is warned the moment the bubbles touch.

In development: a warning before the paths meet, not only on entry.

4Warning

It tells the driver.

The driver is warned in the cab. Crews elsewhere on the apron are not disturbed.

Vision: workers on foot will be warned too.

Who is told: the driver. Nobody else.

What you get

Drivers warned about workers on foot, even out of sight, on the phones they already carry.

The pilot runs on its own, with no connection to your airport’s systems. It works alongside surface surveillance and is never a substitute for clearance.
